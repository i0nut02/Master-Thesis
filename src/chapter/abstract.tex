\begin{abstract}
  This thesis investigates approximate nearest-neighbor (ANN) search as a new application domain for the Tenstorrent architecture and presents, to the best of our knowledge, its first ANN implementation.
  The main ANN index families are first analyzed to identify the one that best matches Tenstorrent's tile-based spatial architecture. Based on this analysis, an IVF-Flat search pipeline is implemented on a Wormhole n150, combining device-side coarse search, host-side scheduling of the lists selected by each 32-query batch, and distributed fine search with hierarchical top-\(k\) selection. On the glove-100-angular dataset, it is compared at matched Recall@10 with Faiss on 56- and 112-core CPU configurations and on an NVIDIA A100 system.
  The A100 achieves the highest throughput throughout, from 31.9 times that of Tenstorrent at 60\% recall to 7.8 times at 95\%. Tenstorrent is slower than both CPU configurations at low recall, but its throughput declines more slowly as search depth grows: at 95\% recall it is 1.74 times faster than the 56-core CPU and on par with the 112-core CPU against the evaluated Faiss grid. For a complete 100,000-query workload including initialization, the A100 system uses the least energy at every recall target. The Tenstorrent system uses 2.3--3.8 times as much as the A100 system but 53--79\% less than the CPU configurations, and its energy is dominated by its host, with the Wormhole device accounting for only 11--18\% of it. Fine search accounts for 90--98\% of the Tenstorrent search time at 512 lists, identifying the per-list data-movement and compute loop as the main target for future optimization.
\end{abstract}
